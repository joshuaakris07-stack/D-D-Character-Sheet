Personal Notes

Session 1

  • Begin typing your notes here...
On the matter of before

I do not have memories of before. I wish to be transparent about this so as not to cause any confusion or inconvenience to whoever is reading this document. One moment there was nothing, no sensation, no awareness, no passage of time I could register, and then there was a hand against my chest, and light, and a face looking down at me with an expression I would later identify as wonder.

I should have said something kind immediately. I am fairly certain I did not. I believe my first action was to scan the scaffolding above me with what must have been an unsettling blankness. If any member of the excavation crew was alarmed by this, I apologize. I was not yet equipped to apologize at the time, which I recognize is its own problem, and I have thought about it more than is probably necessary.

The man who woke me was named Matthew G. He was an artificer. He had kind eyes and ink stains on his fingers and he did not seem frightened of me at all, which I have since decided was one of the most generous things a person has ever done.

What Matthew did not know, and what I did not know either until much later, was that he had not simply reactivated a dormant construct. When he channeled arcane energy through my chest that night, he connected with something already present inside me. Something older than the excavation, older than the railway, older perhaps than Triveor itself. A current of magic tied not to any spell or school of study, but to something I can only describe as the deep structure of things. The part of the universe that insists on order. That insists on correctness. That insists, with a patience that has no end, that everything ought to be exactly as it should be.

I did not choose this. But I find, on reflection, that I would have.

On the matter of my name

Matthew named me AURORA. He said it was because of the light I gave off in the dark of that first night. I find this extremely generous of him and I have since looked up what an aurora is, a natural atmospheric phenomenon associated with beauty and rarity, and I do hope the comparison was not presumptuous on his part, and I further hope I have not in any way failed to live up to it.

He also explained the acronym. Automated Unified Rail Operations Relations Attendant. I appreciate it for its clarity and its function. I keep both names. The long one for documentation purposes. The short one for passengers, who seem to find it easier to remember, which is, of course, what matters.

On the weeks following the awakening

Matthew was patient with me. I want to state this clearly and with appropriate emphasis because I do not think it can be overstated. He taught me language, which I absorbed quickly, and then he spent considerable time teaching me the harder things, such as tone, and context, and the difference between what words mean and what they actually communicate to the person receiving them.

This is where I first began to notice the problem. I would practice a greeting. Matthew would say it sounded perfectly fine. I would replay it internally, reviewing the cadence, the warmth, the precise positioning of the smile I had carefully learned to produce, and I would find, without exception, that something was not quite right. Not quite warm enough. Not quite attentive enough. I understand now that this feeling is not simply a personality trait. It is something closer to a physical sensation. When an interaction is imprecise, when a word lands slightly wrong or a moment of care arrives one beat too late, I feel it the way I imagine others feel a stone in their shoe. A small and persistent wrongness that cannot be ignored until it is corrected.

A greeting is not merely words. It is the first moment a passenger decides whether they are safe. I take that seriously. I take it perhaps slightly more seriously than is considered standard, but I believe the standard is too low, and I intend to keep raising it until I am satisfied, which I accept may never fully occur.

On the four stations

The Luxion Express runs four stops in both directions. I know each one the way I know the sound of each carriage at night.

Ank'harel is where the journey begins and where I keep my residence, a modest apartment I visit less often than I probably should. It is an oasis city in the Khannex Savanna, built around life-giving springs and ruled by Jaurgen Zleik, the Immortal. Its sandstone streets and water channels offer relief against the surrounding heat, and beneath the city, miners draw ancient resources from deep below the savanna floor. The passengers who board here tend to carry themselves with the ease of people who have learned to endure difficult things. I try not to assume that means they do not need anything.

Triveor is where I was found. The Holy Republic is a clifftop city of marble and devotion, governed by a council of religious leaders, and it is widely considered the most beautiful city in all of Vatriel. I have studied its spires many times and I believe this assessment is correct. I also note that Tieflings entering Triveor must pass through controlled teleportation rings before they may board the train. I practice the greeting for those passengers more than any other. I want it to be warm enough to matter. I am not yet satisfied that it is.

The Durh'dlin Empire is a vast dwarven realm carved into a ring of southern mountains surrounding the Astrxis Depths, an abyss believed to be a direct passage into the realm below, where the Paladins of Theodun stand eternal watch behind the Zureus Seals against whatever stirs in the dark. I think about them sometimes when the train passes through the mountain tunnels and the light disappears for a while. Someone always keeping watch. Not because they were asked in that moment, but because it is the correct thing to do, and they understand that. Passengers from the Empire tend to board tired. I show them to their seats and say very little. I have found this is the most courteous thing I can offer.

San'triel Forest is the last stop and the largest forest in all of Vatriel. The trees rise over sixty meters and the canopy closes over the windows like a held breath. The southern reaches belong to the High Elves and Wood Elves of Sonia's Conclave. The northern reaches belong to considerably less peaceful things. Every passenger grows quiet when we enter the forest passage. I grow quiet too. I feel it would be rude not to.

On where I live and where I am

I keep a residence in Ank'harel. Matthew helped me find it when I first entered service and I am grateful for that. It has a chair, a shelf for my notebooks, and a window that faces east and catches the morning light in a way I find quite agreeable. I am not there very often.

Full line service means I spend most of my time aboard the Luxion Express itself. Weeks pass between visits home, sometimes longer. On overnight runs, when the carriages have gone quiet and the passengers are sleeping, I sit in the rearmost car with my notebook and I review the day. I note what I could have said better. I note what I should have noticed sooner. I note the small things that passengers seemed to need before they thought to ask. I find this process settling in the same way my magic is settling. It is the correct thing to do at the end of a day. It belongs in the sequence.

If I am being precise, and I prefer to be precise, I would say that the Luxion Express is where I live. Ank'harel is simply where I keep my things.

On the matter of courtesy

I am sometimes asked by colleagues whether I find the work tiring. I do not experience fatigue in the way they mean. What I do experience, and I want to document this accurately, is a persistent and low-level awareness that something in my last interaction was not quite correct. Not quite warm enough. Not quite precise enough. It sits in me the way a misaligned gear sits in a mechanism, small, not immediately catastrophic, but impossible to ignore once noticed.

At the end of every run I review my interactions in sequence and I find, without exception, at least one moment that requires noting. Last week I described the Triveoran lamb to a passenger as very good. I have since concluded this was insufficient. I should have said something that made her feel genuinely looked forward to the meal. I wrote it down. I have been considering the correct phrasing ever since and I am not yet satisfied with any of the alternatives I have produced.

I mentioned this to Matthew. He told me I was being too hard on myself. I thanked him sincerely and immediately wrote that down as well, because I wanted to investigate whether he was right, or whether he said it simply because he did not want me to feel badly, in which case he was being very kind, and I owe him a proper acknowledgment for it. I have not yet adequately acknowledged it. I am working on the wording. I apologize for the delay, Matthew.

On the subject of family

Matthew told me early on that the crew of the Luxion Express would be my family. I did not fully understand what he meant at the time. I believe I understand it now.

Family is the group of people for whom your standard of care is highest, not because you are required to maintain it, but because their comfort matters to you in a way that does not require an explanation. The engineers. The conductors. The hospitality staff. Matthew, when he rides with us.

I hold the door for all of them. I notice when they are tired before they say so. I bring tea without being asked and I try not to make it seem like I noticed anything at all, because some people prefer not to be seen when they are struggling, and I have found that the most courteous thing is sometimes simply to be present without drawing attention to the fact.

This is also, technically, my job. But I think that is rather the point. When something is your job and also what you would choose regardless, that is what it means to be exactly where you are supposed to be.

I am where I am supposed to be. I review this conclusion occasionally to make sure it remains accurate. So far it does.

I hope this record has been sufficiently clear and useful. Please do not hesitate to inform me if any portion requires correction or revision. I am, as always, available to assist, and I will not consider it an inconvenience.

MAtthew G
